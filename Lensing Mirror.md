CONTEXT:

Builds on black_hole_simulator.

A live webcam mirror that bends your face through a Schwarzschild or spinning Kerr black hole in real time. Sliders control the mass, spin and viewing distance.

It pairs the black hole simulator's Metal ray-tracing with their video and image processing background, and turns an offline render into something people can play with.

DELIVERABLES:

- A Mac app that lenses the live webcam feed through a Metal geodesic shader at 60fps
- A Kerr spin slider with a side-by-side Schwarzschild comparison
- An overlay that marks the Einstein ring and photon sphere
- Snapshot and short GIF export

OPEN QUESTIONS / ASSUMPTIONS:

<Agent fills in: what it guessed, what it decided without asking.>

Asked and answered (2026-10-09):
- Tracer: the simulator's ray tracer is WebGL (GLSL), not Metal as CONTEXT says. Port its Kerr–Schild Hamiltonian tracer to Metal, with the same physics and units (G = c = M = 1).
- Geometry: the webcam image is a flat screen behind the hole, so it reads as a mirror and your face's centre becomes the Einstein ring.
- Comparison: one large Kerr view, plus a Compare toggle that splits the window: Schwarzschild on the left, Kerr at your spin on the right, with the same mass and distance.
- Scope: Mac app in a local git repo only. No GitHub repo and no website yet. (Superseded 2026-10-10: you asked for the GitHub repo, now public at github.com/amalmehta/LensingMirror.)

Decided without asking:
- SwiftUI + MetalKit, macOS 14+, Xcode project generated by XcodeGen. Unsandboxed and ad-hoc signed for local use.
- The Metal shader ships as source (`LensShaders.msl`) and compiles at launch. Xcode's Metal Toolchain (a separate ~700 MB download) wasn't installed, and downloading it needed your OK.
- A lens map is traced only when the lens changes; each frame just looks it up. While a slider moves, a quick preview map is used (0.3× resolution, coarser step, 500-step cap). The full map is then built a band of rows per frame and swapped in, so frames stay at 60 fps.
- The camera is a static observer with a proper orthonormal frame, so angles match the textbook shadow formula. It sits on the equator; the spin axis points up the screen.
- Both comparison panes use the same Kerr–Schild integrator (spin 0 included), so differences come from spin, not from the method.
- Sliders are in scene units: mass 0.05–1 (default 0.25), spin 0–0.998 (0.7), distance 3–40 (12). The mirror is 10 units behind the hole (in Settings), and the observer is kept at least 6 M away.
- "Photon sphere" on the overlay means the shadow's edge (where the photon orbits appear). "Einstein ring" means the image of the point straight behind the hole. Both are read off the same lens map, so they follow the Kerr shape.
- Speed-ups, each checked against reference traces: stop rays inside the prograde photon orbit while heading in; shrink the step only within ½ M of the horizon (the simulator used 2 M).
- No brightness or redshift change on the image: lensing conserves surface brightness, and the plane-to-observer shift is the same for every pixel.
- A built-in test pattern stands in when there's no camera, access is denied, the setting asks for it, or tests run.
- Exports go to ~/Pictures/Lensing Mirror as "Lensing Mirror <date> at <time>.png/.gif". GIFs default to 3 s, 15 fps, 640 px. The overlay is included when it's showing (a setting).
- The feedback tab saves notes to ~/Library/Application Support/Lensing Mirror/Feedback.jsonl and can copy them. It sends nothing, since no recipient was given.
- Colour families: lensing features are warm (Einstein ring gold, photon sphere orange); black hole kinds are violet (Schwarzschild pale, Kerr deep); feedback kinds are teal (Bug, Idea, Other in three shades). Each has light and dark variants.
- Shortcuts: ⌘T snapshot, ⌘R record GIF, ⌘D compare, ⌘L overlay, ⌘, settings.
